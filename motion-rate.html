Compare animation travel in consistent units

A clip that travels two metres in 1.25 seconds has a different average movement rate when it is played at a different speed. Before comparing two motion previews, normalize distance and time. Then review how the motion actually looks in the game.

Try the arithmetic

This calculation assumes a positive, constant playback multiplier. The entered distance is travel over the full original clip duration.

200 cm / (1.25 s / 1.25) = 2 m/s
Arithmetic only. Gameplay acceptance: not assessed.

Where the numbers come from

Convert distance to metres first: 200 cm becomes 2 m. Divide the original duration by the playback multiplier: 1.25 seconds / 1.25 becomes 1 second. Divide distance by that effective duration: 2 m / 1 s becomes 2 m/s.

At normal playback, the same example averages 1.6 m/s. Increasing the multiplier changes the duration in this model; it does not change the distance travelled over the whole clip. These are arithmetic examples, not measurements of a shipped game.

Frame counts need a convention

If a duration is provided in frame intervals, divide the number of intervals by the sampling rate. Sixty intervals at 30 intervals per second represent two seconds. A list of 60 sample positions from the first to the last has 59 intervening intervals. Write down which convention your export uses before comparing durations.

A matching mean is only one question

Two clips can have the same average travel and different acceleration, foot contacts, pauses or turning. An in-place clip may have zero root displacement while the character moves through gameplay code. Blending, transition timing, controller behavior and collision also need their own review.

Use this tool to expose a unit or duration mismatch. It does not establish correct root motion, prevent sliding or accept the animation. The broader motion-package commissioning guide explains the build and handover discussion around those checks.
